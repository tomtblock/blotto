## 2. The seal, then the share — what moved and why

**Before this change.** The review beat of the battle run (after the seventh lock-in, before the
wallet signature) composed a labelled montage of the seven Blotts and offered Share / Post on X.
The profile grid offered Share on every still. Both rested on *a still shows a shape, not numbers,
so it leaks nothing*.

**Why that was wrong.** The Blott is drawn from its allocation: each stat warps one feature of the
silhouette, monotonically (`components/blott/warp.ts`). Given the sprite, the five numbers are
recoverable to within the eye's resolution — and a montage labelled 1…7 hands an opponent the
sealed line-up, in order, before the pool closes. Sealing is a property of the mechanism
(`CLAUDE.md`: *sealing is a property, not a promise*); a share button that publishes the plaintext
defeats it as surely as a backend that reads it early would.

**After this change.**

| Moment | What is offered | Why it is safe |
|---|---|---|
| Review beat, before the signature | Nothing to share. A `Card` with the padlock (`public/candy/lock.webp`) and two lines: what sealing is, and that the pass comes after | — |
| `tx_confirmed` → the `card` beat | **The character pass** — class, rarity, seat, pool number, the Blott sprite. Share (Web Share with the file; else download and copy the line) and Post on X | `RevealOutcome` carries no allocation by construction (`reveal/types.ts`); the card cannot draw what it never receives |
| A CLOSED pool's results page | **The seven-Blott montage** (`pools/[pool]/results/share-seven.tsx`), only when the pool's state is not `Open` and all seven stills for that pool exist on this device | The battles are published at close; the montage adds nothing the Verify page does not already show |
| Profile grid | The stills, no share; a note pointing at the results page | The grid holds stills from pools that may still be Open |

The pass is composed on the device (`play/compose-pass.ts`: pink field, cream card with the berry
rim, a halo in the class hue, wordmark · pool line, headline, class, rarity, seat, the 18+ and
territory footer) with every colour taken from `design/tokens.ts` — no hex literals, so the tokens
parity test still holds. The review beat still renders and saves the seven stills to IndexedDB
exactly as before; only their publication moved. The header comment of `battle/montage.ts` now says
when a montage may be offered and names the wrong inference, so the next reader does not repeat it.

## 3. Surface by surface

### 3.1 The candy material — light on a surface, not new colours

The home page already had the look; the rest of the site was flat. The gap was not colour — the
two-layer palette (brand pink and yellow; game plum) and its reservations (pink commits; prize
yellow is a paid amount and nothing else; class hues are art) are unchanged — it was **light**. The
material is five box-shadow recipes in `styles/tokens.css` (`--elevation-candy`, `-hover`, `-light`,
`--elevation-key-action[-down]`, `--elevation-key-prize[-down]`; mirrored in `design/tokens.ts`):
a top-lit gradient, a cream inset rim, a berry underside, a berry-tinted drop. They are applied
through four classes — `candy`, `candy-light`, `candy-interactive`, `candy-gloss` — and two "key"
classes for buttons, whose bottom edge sinks 3 px on `:active`, like a key on a keyboard.

| Primitive | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| `ui/card.tsx` | flat plum rectangle, one border | `tone="plum"` (top-lit plum gradient, 24 px radius, gloss) or `tone="light"` (cream on berry); `live` keeps the brand rim; `interactive` lifts 2 px on hover |
| `ui/button.tsx` | flat fills | primary and prize are keys (`key-action`, `key-prize`); `lg` is a display-face, uppercase call to action |
| `ui/pill.tsx` (new) | ad-hoc status spans | one pill, four tones — `open`, `resolving`, `muted`, `ink`. There is deliberately no prize tone |
| `ui/back-link.tsx` (new) | none | chevron and label, a real link |
| `ui/page-intro.tsx` (new) | each page hand-set its own heading | `{title, lead?, back?, aside?}` — the same frame on every leaf |
| `shell/site-footer.tsx` (new) | none | the 18+ and territory note, and the four reference links: Rules · Fair play · Verify · Custody |

### 3.2 Navigation and accessibility — no dead ends

- **Skip link** (`shell/skip-link.tsx`): first element in `<body>`, visible on focus, targets `#main`.
- **Route focus** (`shell/route-focus.tsx`): on every navigation, focus moves to `#main` without
  scrolling, so a screen reader announces the new page instead of staying on the link that was
  pressed. Skipped on the first render.
- **One focus ring** (`app/globals.css`): every link, button, radio, summary and tabbable element
  shows the same cream 2 px outline on `:focus-visible`. Before, half the controls showed none.
- **`id="main"`** on all nineteen `<main>` elements — the skip link and the route focus need it.
- **Every leaf has a way back and a way home**: a `BackLink` in the intro (rules → play, pool →
  pools, results → pool, custody → wallet, …), the `SiteFooter` below, and "Back to homepage" on
  both the sealed panel and the pass. The three nav-less pages (sign-in, the gate, the reveal
  takeover) are nav-less on purpose and were left so.
- **Sign-in** lands on the home page (§0).

## 4. Dial locks — the rule, and worked examples

**The ask.** *If I set the first skill as 20/100 and lock it, the remaining four skills have a
maximum of 80 to distribute. This is reflected in the sliding dials.*

**The rule** (`components/blot/rebalance.ts`, `rebalanceLocked`):

1. A locked stat never moves — not by drag, not by give-way. Its dial is disabled and dimmed.
2. Dragging stat *k* to *t*: the ceiling is `100 − Σ(locked others)`, and *t* is clamped to it. The
   remainder `ceiling − t` is re-apportioned across the UNLOCKED others in proportion to what they
   held, with largest-remainder rounding, so the five still sum to exactly 100. The allocation
   invariant is enforced here — in the client, as `CLAUDE.md` says it must be — never on-chain.
3. With four stats locked the fifth is determined; its dial is pinned rather than left to snap back.
4. "Even split" clears the locks together with the shape — it returns the screen to the state it
   opened in.
5. Locks do not carry to the next battle; every screen opens unlocked, at the even split.

**How the budget is shown.** As RANGE, never as a number: each unlocked dial's `max` is its ceiling,
the track beyond it is drawn shaded, `aria-valuemax` follows, and End goes to the ceiling. PID §4.2
forbids a points-remaining counter; this respects it while making the founder's "80 to distribute"
visible.

**Worked examples** (stat order Strength · Speed · Heart · Wit · Durability; bold = locked):

| Start | Lock | Drag | Result | Range of the dragged dial |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 · 20 · 20 · 20 · 20 | Strength | Speed → 50 | **20** · 50 · 10 · 10 · 10 | 0–80 |
| 20 · 20 · 20 · 20 · 20 | Strength | Speed → 80 | **20** · 80 · 0 · 0 · 0 | 0–80 |
| 20 · 20 · 20 · 20 · 20 | Strength, Heart | Wit → 60 | **20** · 0 · **20** · 60 · 0 | 0–60 |
| 20 · 20 · 20 · 20 · 20 | Strength, Speed, Heart, Wit | Durability is pinned at 20 | unchanged | — |

Tests: `components/blot/rebalance.test.ts` — the founder's example, no-op on a locked stat, the
four-locked case, the cap, the all-zero equal share, and ten thousand random allocations under
random locks, each asserting the sum, the locked values and the ceiling; `battle/machine.test.ts` —
`toggle_lock` as an event, locks cleared on even split and on the next screen, expiry unaffected.

**One judgement call, `PHASE-N-REVIEW` (`battle/machine.ts`, `toggle_lock`):** a lock starts the
ninety-second run clock, exactly as the first drag does. A lock is a decision about the allocation,
and the alternative — free planning time by locking before the first drag — is a loophole in the
ninety seconds. The founder may rule otherwise; it is one line to change.

### 6.2 The on-ramp — three options, one recommendation

**What exists.** "Add SOL" calls Privy's `fundWallet` with `defaultFundingMethod: 'manual'`
(`wallet/privy-bridge.tsx`), which opens Privy's deposit-address screen — IF funding is switched on
in the Privy dashboard, and the go-live checklist says *Leave Funding off* (`go-live-devnet.md`).
The client's own top-up prompt sits behind `NEXT_PUBLIC_TOPUP_ENABLED`, which register row D-C
ships defaulting OFF. No fiat provider is wired anywhere in the repository. The wallet card
carries the devnet faucet link behind its build switch.

**The devnet answer, plainly: fiat-to-SOL cannot be tested on devnet.** No provider sells devnet
SOL — the tokens are free from the faucet and worth nothing, so there is nothing to buy. MoonPay's
sandbox shows its card form against test cards but delivers no devnet SOL to the wallet, and the
sandbox flag is neither configured nor verified here. What CAN be tested on devnet is the
deposit-address flow (option a).

| Option | What it is | Cost | Who decides |
|---|---|---|---|
| **(a) Turn Privy funding on now** | The deposit-address screen works on devnet today; testers fund from the faucet or another wallet. Zero code | A dashboard toggle | Founder — **recommended for the devnet test period** |
| (b) Card funding on mainnet through Privy's MoonPay or Coinbase integration | Real fiat → real SOL into the embedded wallet | A provider account, a KYC surface, terms; a small code change | **Founder and counsel** — money-in and KYC-adjacent, and it reverses D-C's flag-off default. Goes to counsel before any code |
| (c) The MoonPay sandbox | See the card form on devnet with nothing delivered | A sandbox key and a build flag | Founder — only worth it to preview the screen |

Rejected: wiring a provider in this PR on the assumption that (b) will be approved. It would put a
money-in surface on a branch the founder has not ruled on.

### 6.5 Noted, not fixed — out of this change's scope

Two deploy paths, one gate. The program upgrade is drain-gated (`scripts/deploy-devnet.sh`: drain,
pause, upgrade, migrate, unpause — `ARCHITECTURE-DIAGRAMS.md`; the verdict is
`scripts/drain-verdict.ts`, run by the founder through `admin.ts pools` and only self-tested in CI's
standing-rules lane). The backend deploy is not: `.github/workflows/deploy-backend.yml` has no drain
step, and `go-live-devnet.md` §7 relies on a mid-settlement pool resuming. As far as the code shows
that asymmetry is by design — the daemon is idempotent, the upgrade is not — but no document states
it as a rule. The next docs pass should, so that nobody adds a gate the daemon does not need or
removes the one the upgrade does.
