## The point predictions, which are what make T3 sharper than T5 and T6

T5 and T6 could only predict the wall's score as a **range**, because the founders' allocations
swung it. Premise P1 — the wall holds three stats at 26+ in every battle, no capped seat holds any
above 25 — fixes **both** of the wall's founder battles, so T3 predicted a **point**:


Neither is a range. Both landed exactly.

## Why the reconcile says FAIL, and why it is not the product

**22 of 38 headless seats drifted in rank.** Exact field ranks are **not** a pass criterion — the
seventeen above are.

The cause is §H10 for the **third time**, and it is a defect in my plan, not in the program. The
plan marked only the two founder seats `rankIsConditional`. It should have marked all 38 headless
seats conditional too, because **every one of their ranks depends on how the founders played**. T4
had it on 2 seats, T5 on 44 of 62, T3 on 22 of 38. Three pools, one lesson, not yet learned in the
artifact that keeps getting it wrong.

## What the founders actually played, and why it matters

This is the finding of the run, and it was **invisible in the first reconcile**:

```
UI-B   all seven battles   [20,20,20,20,20]
UI-A   battles 1-2 dialled, battles 3-7   [20,20,20,20,20]
```

**The even split is the timeout default** — what the client submits when the ninety-second clock
runs out. UI-B never moved a dial on any screen. UI-A started, then let the timer expire on five of
seven. That is **§H13 from T5 recurring**, and harder: there it was one seat, here it is both, one
of them completely.

**Every unconditional claim held anyway, and that is the result worth keeping.** An even split is
five non-zero stats, none above 25 — so P1 and P2 both still applied, and the plan's two-tier split
did exactly the job it exists to do:

- the **conditional** layer was wrong: 33 of 40 scores differ from the plan;
- the **unconditional** layer was exactly right: W00 542 and rank 1, W01 4 and rank 40, neither
  founder seat first or last.

A prediction that survives both founders playing nothing like the recipe is a stronger result than
one that survives them following it.

Two, both recorded, neither the product's:

1. **Base64 into a base58 field.** Squads' TX Builder Data field is base58; `admin.ts
   --emit-proposal` prints `dataBase64`, and an "Export as base58" button sat on the same screen.
   The program threw `InstructionFallbackNotFound` (101) on nine bytes that matched no

2. **No freeze record was written at freeze time.** `reconcile.ts` refused the pool as UNFROZEN and
   called it a FAIL by itself. It was right. `T3.freeze.json` now exists, built from GitHub's merge
   timestamp, git hashes and devnet block times — and it states plainly that it was written two
   days late, and that **T3 holds the weaker freeze standard**: frozen before any entry landed
   (1 h 33 min), but **not** before the pool existed, since pool 29 was opened 2 h 30 min before
   the plan merged. T6 and T2R hold the stronger one. This does not.
